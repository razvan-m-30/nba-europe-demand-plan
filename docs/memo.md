## Why analog events

NBA Europe has no demand history: a sustained NBA-backed league has never operated in Europe. To estimate how demand may respond, this plan measures how public interest in basketball reacted to three comparable past events. For each one it records the size of the uplift, how quickly it faded and how much of it persisted. Those three measures then set the low, base and high demand scenarios.

The three events were chosen because each captures a different mechanism through which a league could lift demand.

| Event | Date | Markets most exposed | What it represents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Victor Wembanyama drafted #1 overall** | 22 June 2023 | France | A star-driven shock followed by ongoing weekly exposure as the player competes all season |
| **Paris 2024 Olympic basketball** | 27 July – 11 August 2024 | France (host and finalist in both tournaments); other participating nations | A high-visibility, multi-week tournament |
| **NBA regular-season games in Europe** | Paris: 23 & 25 January 2025 · Berlin: 15 January 2026 · London: 18 January 2026 | France, Germany, UK | Live NBA competition on European soil: the closest match to the league itself |

## Reading the results with care

Each analog carries a known bias, and the analysis corrects for it where possible:

- **The star effect.** The 2025 Paris games were Wembanyama's homecoming, and the 2026 Berlin game featured Germany's Franz and Moritz Wagner. Some of the local uplift therefore comes from national stars rather than from the NBA itself.
- **Olympic halo.** Interest during Paris 2024 was lifted by the Games as a whole, and by France reaching both finals. This analog is best treated as an upper bound.
- **Seasonality.** The draft falls in the off-season, while the European games fall mid-season. All uplifts are measured against each market's own pre-event baseline, and against the Netherlands as a control market with no planned franchise.

## Forward context

The NBA has already committed to regular-season games in Manchester and Paris in 2027 and in Berlin and Paris in 2028. Demand in these markets is therefore likely to be lifted by one-off games alongside the league launch. The plan accounts for this rather than attributing all uplift to the league.
